Freelance consultant monthly bookkeeping: AI-native redesign

Prepared with Reprocess v0.1 on 2026-10-28 (example) · Scope: One person; monthly, quarterly and year-end bookkeeping · Draft for discussion, not a validated design.

1. Executive summary

  • Most of the work is predictable: 6 of 10 future steps need no AI, only rules, imports and integrations.
  • AI helps in 2 places, receipt extraction and categorizing transactions that rules miss, and a human confirms both.
  • Estimate: about 6.4 h/month today drops to 1.8 to 2.8 h/month, but self-hosting adds 1 to 2 h/month of upkeep. Net saving is about 1.7 to 3.7 h/month.
  • A simpler option with no AI and no server (Option 2) gets roughly half the benefit with no upkeep. It is a valid choice.
  • Start with a pilot of bank import, rules and receipt matching. That step has the highest saving and lowest risk.

6. Build options

Recommended: self-hosted stack on your NAS (Docker)

  • Documents and OCR: Paperless-ngx (GPL-3.0). It has a watched email inbox and folder, and auto-tags vendors.
  • Bookkeeping and rules: Firefly III (AGPL-3.0) with its CSV data importer. Its rules engine handles step 5a.
  • Orchestration: n8n (Sustainable Use License, source-available, free for internal use). It links Paperless, the LLM and Firefly III, and runs the monthly batches and approval emails.
  • AI model: Ollama (MIT) running a small open-weight model of about 7 to 8B parameters on the NAS. Volume is small, about 40 receipts a month, so slow inference is fine. Data stays on your machine.
  • Invoicing and reminders: Invoice Ninja (source-available, verify license) with automatic payment reminders. Or keep your Word template and let n8n email you a draft for approval.

Why it fits: you are comfortable with low-code, have a home server and want data local. Effort: M, about 2 to 4 days of setup and tuning. Running cost: electricity and your time. Risks: a small local model will misread some receipts, so keep G2. 16 GB RAM is tight for running everything at once; run the LLM batch alone. Maintenance: 1 to 2 h/month for updates and checking backups. Financial data needs encrypted, tested backups.

Option 2: no server, no AI (GnuCash desktop)

  • GnuCash (GPL-2.0+). Its CSV import learns your categories over time, so it learns step 4 without an LLM.
  • Gmail filter forwards receipts to one label. A phone scan app saves to one synced folder.
  • Calendar reminders for chasing payments and tax dates.

Effort: S, about half a day. Saving: about 1.5 to 2.5 h/month. Categorizing and receipt collection improve; matching stays manual. Maintenance: almost none. Trade-off: less automation, but nothing to run or secure. Choose this if you don't want to look after a server.

Option 3: ERPNext, if you plan to grow or hire

ERPNext (GPL-3.0) covers invoicing, payment reminders, bank reconciliation and accounting in one system. Add Paperless-ngx for receipts. Effort: L. It is overkill for one person today, but worth it if you expect staff or more than about 15 clients.

7. Rollout plan and metrics

  1. Weeks 1 to 2, pilot: bank import, recurring-vendor rules and auto-matching (steps 4, 5a, 6). Highest saving, no AI risk.
  2. Weeks 3 to 4: receipt collection into Paperless (2), then AI extraction with gate G2 (3).
  3. Month 2: AI category suggestions with gate G3 (5b), and invoices with reminders (1, 7).
  4. Month 3: review the metrics and decide whether G2 can move to exception-only review.

Track monthly: total bookkeeping minutes, share of transactions categorized by rules, AI correction rate (extraction and category), unmatched receipts at month end, days to payment on invoices.

Estimate arithmetic: future step times are 10 to 15 (invoices), 10 to 15 (receipts), 10 to 20 (extraction review), 5 (import), 15 to 30 (categorizing), 10 to 20 (matching exceptions), 10 (chasing), 15 to 20 (review), 15 to 20 (tax), 5 to 10 (year-end) min/month. That totals 105 to 165 min, against 385 min today. Subtract 60 to 120 min/month of upkeep for a net saving of about 100 to 220 min/month. These are estimates; measure them in the pilot.

8. Limitations and how to improve

Limitations of this analysis

  • Based only on the SOP above; no access to your files or bank.
  • Tax rules depend on your country and are not assessed here. Your accountant stays the authority.
  • Future step times are estimates, not measurements.
  • Tool details and licenses may have changed since this was written.

Limitations of the proposed system

  • Local small models misread blurry photos, handwritten receipts and foreign-language receipts more often than large hosted models.
  • The AI can suggest a plausible but wrong tax category. G3 is there for that reason.
  • Rules break when vendors change their billing names; review rule misses monthly.
  • Bank CSV formats change without notice, and the import will fail until you remap the columns.
  • Self-hosting makes you the IT department: updates, backups and security.

Information that would improve this advice

  • Your country and bank. Automatic bank feeds may replace CSV downloads.
  • The share of transactions that are recurring vendors, to firm up the 5a estimate.
  • Whether your accountant accepts a specific export format (this could remove step 10).
  • How many receipts are paper versus email.

What to tune once running

  • Add a rule each time you correct the same vendor twice.
  • Give the extraction prompt 5 to 10 examples of your real receipts.
  • Adjust the G3 amount threshold to your comfort level.
  • Try a larger model if the extraction correction rate stays above 5%.
